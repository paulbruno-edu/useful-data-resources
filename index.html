Useful Data Resources

Free data sources for quantitative research, collected with doctoral students in education in mind. Most are about K-12 and higher education in the United States; some are general social science sources that work well for methods practice or for linking to education data.

Start here

If you're new to education data, these widely used national sources are good places to start looking. Whether one fits depends on your question, and some take real preparation to use. Several share NCES school, district, or college IDs, so they can be linked to each other. The getting started guide covers access, restricted-use data, and linking sources.

    About this list

    What's included. Sources whose data are free to download or free to request. A few are free to explore but have more detailed versions that need an application, and some of those charge fees. Descriptions in quotation marks are from the publisher.

    Access labels. Open download: free, no account needed. Free registration: free after creating an account and agreeing to terms. Mixed: free public files, plus more detailed files that need an application or license. Application or restricted: available only through an application, license, or contract. The getting started guide says more.

    How it's kept current. Every link is checked automatically once a month, and moved or broken links are fixed by hand. Checking a link doesn't confirm that a source's contents or access rules are unchanged, so descriptions are also reviewed by hand from time to time. Each entry also has an "archived" link to the Internet Archive's most recent copy, in case a page moves or goes offline.

    Caveats. Access rules and units of analysis were recorded from each source's main page and can change; check the source's documentation before you plan around them.
